# REVIEW.md: self-review as a critical referee

## Summary (3 sentences)
The manuscript reproduces the corruption model of Lee et al. (2019), checked against the main
text and the SI Appendix, and extends it with term-limited umpires whose newcomers may inherit the integrity of incumbents. The
main analytical claim is that rotation removes corruption cycles through a saddle-node on the
limit cycle at a critical rate rho_c = 0.0133 (mean field), approximated within 2.5% by
a formula proportional to the exploration rate. Pre-registered hypotheses are evaluated in the
mean field and in finite populations: H1 supported, H2 not supported, H3 supported,
H4 substitutes (mean field and IBM agree), H5 mixed, H6 not supported.

## Major weaknesses
1. **Individual-based procedure partly assumed.** The SI of Lee et al. states the exploration
   rules and the fixation rules, and its Fig. 8 supports our time unit, but it does not give the
   update order, role-model sampling or finite-population payoffs. These remain assumptions, and
   every finite-population result depends on them. The published ratio 2.15 is matched only
   under one definition of the time average (time all honest / all corrupt 2.134
   [2.102, 2.165]; ratio of mean shares 2.030
   [2.003, 2.057]). *Action taken:* SI checked line by line
   (SI_EXTRACT.md), candidate causes of the B3 gap tested one at a time (CHANGES_SI.md), and the
   remaining assumptions stated in README, paper and SPEC.md.
2. **Limited novelty of fresh-cohort rotation.** In the mean field, V0 rotation is algebraically
   biased exploration of umpires toward honesty, an idea close to enforcer "education"
   (Lee et al. 2015) and enforcer exploration (Zhou et al. 2023). *Action taken:* the equivalence
   is stated in Section 2.2 and the Discussion; novelty is claimed only for the bifurcation
   structure, the inheritance variants, pulses and the finite-population analysis, "to our
   knowledge".
3. **Different time scales across levels.** The mean field is the replicator-mutator system
   (weak-selection form), the small IBM uses hard imitation, and rotation rates are in model time
   in one and in generations in the other. Cross-level comparisons are therefore qualitative.
   *Action taken:* stated in Methods and Limitations; the IBM was validated against its own exact
   mean field (error 0.011 at M = 32000).
4. **Mean-field exploration rate differs from the paper's showcase.** The extension uses
   mu = nu = 0.01 rather than 1e-4. *Action taken:* justified in DECISIONS.md D-013; checked at
   1e-3 and 0.02; rho_c is proportional to mu over the scanned range.
5. **H5 relies partly on a follow-up.** The pre-registered scan found 1 bistable point
   of 240; the open bistable region and the hysteresis loop come from a targeted
   follow-up (E5b) and a post-hoc sweep. *Action taken:* the verdict is "mixed", and pre-registered
   and post-hoc results are reported separately.
6. **Global behavior inferred numerically.** The claim that all trajectories reach the honest
   equilibrium above rho_c rests on three initial states and continuation, not on a proof.
   *Not fixed;* stated in the text ("all three initial states we tried").
7. **Mechanism for H2 not tested.** The explanation that rotation shortens episodes by feeding
   honest umpires into them is plausible but untested. *Action taken:* labeled as such.

## Minor weaknesses
- The episode depth "peak corruption" saturates near 1 in the small population (N = 10), so the
  lowest cooperator share is the more informative depth measure.
- Episodes are defined by a fixed threshold y < 0.5; in the large population no such episodes
  remain for rho >= 0.05, so H2 can only be tested in the small population there.
- Rotation rates up to 0.3 per generation imply very short terms (about 3 generations).
- In the IBM, V1 differs from V0 at rho (1 - kappa) at 2 of 15 points
  (largest difference 0.0073); this is near the expected false-positive count and
  disappears with a Bonferroni adjustment, but the strict pre-registered verdict is "breaks".
- The Hopf points near mu = nu = 0.05 (two-parameter diagram) were located but not analyzed.
- The near-equality of large-population honesty with (nu + rho)/(2 nu + rho) is an empirical
  observation without derivation.
- Figure files are numbered by the planned list; paper figures follow citation order
  (mapping in figures/README.md).
- The main text with captions is close to the upper end of the 5000-7000 word target.

## What the SI verification resolved and what remains
Resolved:
- Exploration: players switch to one of the three other strategies, umpires flip, nu = mu M / N
  (SI p.3, Fig. 7, Fig. 8). Our model already did this.
- Time unit: SI Fig. 8 cycle counts agree with ours at the same settings (within
  6% for s = 10). A per-generation time unit would be off by a factor of
  1200 to 3600.
- Markov chain: the SI fixation rules for neutral mutants explain every printed entry of T
  except D4, and they corrected our own first derivation of one entry (CHANGES_SI.md). The SI's
  cost-free chain is reproduced exactly.
- The SI text supports our readings of the Fig. 3 caption (D3) and of row 3 of T (D4).

Remains:
- Update order (asynchronous random sequential in our model), role-model sampling and
  finite-population payoffs (no self-play) are not stated in the SI.
- The time average behind the published 2.15 is not defined. Our simulation matches 2.15 only if
  it counts time with all umpires honest against time with all umpires corrupt.
- SI Fig. 8 panels with s = 1 show 0% to 38% fewer cycles than
  our model; each SI panel is one run, so this is weak evidence of a difference.
- New notation inconsistency D7: in SI Fig. 9 the axis labels are exchanged relative to the
  caption.
